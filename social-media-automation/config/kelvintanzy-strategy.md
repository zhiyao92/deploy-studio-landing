# KelvinTanZY content strategy

## Editorial promise

Every post must be informative, useful, fact-checked, visually clear, and concise.
Curiosity earns attention, but the post must deliver the answer rather than withhold it.

Technical carousels must make the narrative explicit: problem statement, why the
problem occurs, solution, visual example, when to use it, and an important
limitation or takeaway. Problem and solution must appear on the media itself;
the caption cannot be the only place that supplies context.

## Formats

- Publish once daily at 04:00 Asia/Kuala_Lumpur, with delivery reconciliation at 04:30.
- Use carousels for comparisons, explanations, and saveable frameworks.
- Use a single image only when one idea can be understood in seconds.
- Do not use reels in this stream.
- Covers use 12 words or fewer. Single-image takeaways use 8 words or fewer.
- X is the primary creative target: 70–80% standalone posts, 20–30% short
  explanatory threads only when context genuinely requires multiple steps. Never
  republish carousel labels such as “Slide 1” or “Slide 2”; every post must make
  sense on its own and end with a specific discussion question.

## Illustration and visual variety

- Every post cover must contain an original, topic-specific editorial illustration;
  a plain text-only cover is not publishable. Illustrations should have the warm,
  friendly, rounded feel of the onboarding comparison Kelvin approved.
- The illustration must explain or dramatize the idea (for example, two onboarding
  paths, a passkey unlocking a device, an AI workflow, or a SwiftUI map result),
  not be generic decoration. Use original vector art or clearly labeled conceptual
  UI; do not imply a mock screen is a real product screenshot.
- Vary composition, palette, camera/scene, and storytelling device across posts.
  Do not reuse the same pair of text cards and arrow as the default visual. Mix
  character scenes, annotated comparisons, UI mockups, diagrams, and object-led
  metaphors as appropriate to the topic.
- Keep illustrations legible at phone size, with generous whitespace and little
  or no text inside the art. The post still needs a clear problem, insight, and
  useful takeaway in its slides/caption.
- Every new curated deck must declare its illustration direction and pass the
  render/quality checks before it can enter the daily rotation.

## Editorial mix

Measure the mix across a rolling 20-post window rather than forcing every week
to contain every category:

- 40% successful apps and product strategy
- 25% SwiftUI and Apple APIs, including verified code-and-result examples
- 15% AI products and practical workflows
- 10% general technology and security
- 10% Kelvin's own products and builder lessons

Research-led external posts take priority over routine project updates. A post
may be skipped when there is no useful, primary-source-supported insight; never
publish filler merely to satisfy the percentage target.

## SwiftUI and Apple APIs

- Prefer Apple Developer Documentation or an Apple sample project as the code source.
- Show Apple's documented declaration or a faithful minimal invocation. Never ask AI to invent an API or implementation.
- A runnable example must pass a local compiler/type-check when the build environment supports it.
- Every code slide needs a visual result. Use an actual rendered result or a clearly labeled conceptual diagram; never decorative placeholder art.
- If Apple does not publish enough information for a safe example, publish an API explainer without tutorial code.
- Claude may improve only the hook and discussion question. It may not edit code, factual claims, source links, or result labels.

## Fact policy

- External claims require a dated primary source and claim-level source URL.
- Distinguish documented fact from Kelvin's product or design analysis.
- Avoid performance, availability, security, or superiority claims unless the cited source supports them.

## Caption policy

- Hook, two or three short explanatory paragraphs, one natural question, source, then 3–5 focused hashtags.
- Adapt the opening and length to Instagram, Threads, and X; keep the underlying facts identical.
- Avoid stock headings, fake urgency, unsupported superlatives, and repetitive “I built…” framing.
- Platform adaptation: X is the primary creative target and should use one
  standalone post with a concise hook, builder takeaway, and one discussion
  question. Use at most 1–2 highly relevant hashtags on X (never a hashtag block).
  Threads can use a slightly fuller explanation and 2–3 hashtags. Instagram can
  use the visual plus a longer formatted caption and 3–5 focused hashtags. Keep
  facts and source links identical across platforms, but never copy carousel
  labels or force an X post into a thread.
- Use a rotating hook bank (contrarian observation, curiosity, problem, comparison,
  mistake, result, or question) and do not repeat the same hook pattern or topic
  on consecutive posts. External stories are the starting point, not the content:
  add an original builder lesson and invite a concrete opinion or experience.
